## Quick Summary

A multimeter can answer three basic questions: is a connection complete, does the correct voltage reach a point, and how much current flows through a path? The selected mode and probe sockets change how the meter interacts with a circuit. A particularly dangerous mistake is measuring voltage while the lead remains in a current socket, because the meter may create a short circuit.

This guide concerns low-voltage DC projects such as ESP32, sensors and relay drivers. Do not measure mains without training, correctly rated equipment and competent supervision.

## Inspect before applying power

Check wiring, polarity, connectors and obvious shorts visually. With power removed and capacitors safely discharged, use continuity to check intended ground connections, fuses and conductors. Fluke specifies that continuity should only be tested when voltage is not present.

A continuity beep does not prove the path performs well under load. Contact resistance may become important at higher current, so continue with voltage-drop testing while the low-voltage system operates.

## Measure DC voltage in parallel

Place the black lead in COM and the red lead in the voltage or resistance socket. Select DC voltage and an appropriate range. Voltage is measured in parallel between two points. In an ESP32 project, check the adapter, regulator input, 5 V or 3.3 V rail and the load during the event that causes trouble.

Always record the reference point. Saying that a sensor measured 3.3 V is incomplete without the ground point and load condition.

## Measure current in series

Current measurement requires opening the path and inserting the meter in series. Move the lead to the correct current socket, begin on a high range and check the meter fuse. Never place a meter in current mode directly across a battery or supply because this creates a short through the meter.

Return the red lead to the voltage socket immediately after the measurement. A current sensor or oscilloscope with a suitable shunt may be better for transient or long-running measurements.

## Follow a systematic fault path

Start at the source and move toward the load: adapter, regulator, rail, connector, driver and component. Compare a working condition with the failure condition. Stable power with a wrong signal points toward logic level, configuration or communications; a falling rail points toward the supply path and load.

Photograph probe placement and record mode, range and conditions. This gives report evidence and prevents numbers from losing their context.

## Validation Plan

- Check probe sockets before every measurement.
- Use continuity only with power removed and capacitors discharged.
- Measure supply voltage at idle and peak load.
- Record mode, range, probe points and system condition.

Run tests with one controlled change at a time. Preserve configuration, raw measurements and failed runs rather than only successful screenshots. For a final year project, repeatable evidence is stronger than promotional claims. When the available instrument or method cannot prove a statement, report that limitation and define the additional experiment that would be required.

## Common Mistakes

- Measuring voltage with the lead in a current socket.
- Testing resistance or continuity on a live circuit.
- Treating a beep as proof of a good load connection.
- Working on mains without suitable competence and equipment.

Move these risks into a checklist or project risk register before demo day. A fault found while architecture and wiring are still flexible is normally less expensive to correct than one discovered after hardware, firmware and report chapters depend on the same assumption.

## FAQ

### Why can a meter show 3.3 V while the ESP32 still resets?

A handheld meter may not capture a short transient. Check reset logs, wiring, cable and load, then use an oscilloscope only when it is available and can be operated safely.

### Can I measure current across battery terminals?

No. In current mode that can short the battery through the meter. Current is measured in series with a controlled load and suitable protection.
